## Abstract

We have discovered PSR J2108+5055, an extremely luminous but sporadic mode-changing rotating radio transient (RRAT), with a period of 0.495 s using the Canadian Hydrogen Intensity Mapping Experiment (CHIME) Fast Radio Burst (FRB) instrument. We show that the brightest pulse from PSR J2108+5055 has a flux density of 3.1 ± 1.0 kJy, corresponding to a spectral luminosity of (7.5 ± 2.3) × 10^23 erg s^−1 Hz^−1, assuming a dispersion-measure-derived distance of 1.6 ± 0.3 kpc. We place an upper limit on the duty cycle of PSR J2108+5055 at 0.002 and measure a surface magnetic field of 5.8 × 10^11 G, a characteristic age of 1.2 × 10^7 yr, and a spindown luminosity of 2.2 × 10^32 erg s^−1. Using a simple model based on the Green Bank North Celestial Cap (GBNCC) survey, we place an upper limit on the number of PSR J2108+5055–like sources with similar peak flux densities in the GBNCC survey area of ∼80 RRATs. We show that if PSR J2108+5055 were placed at the edge of the Local Group of galaxies, the Five-hundred-meter Aperture Spherical radio Telescope (FAST) would detect its brightest pulse. The second brightest pulse has a peak flux density of 1.5 ± 0.7 kJy, and FAST would detect such a pulse out to ∼1 Mpc, covering the inner Local Group, including Andromeda.
